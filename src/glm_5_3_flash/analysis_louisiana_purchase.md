# Video Analysis: How America Bought Louisiana — Napoleon's $15M Deal

> Analyzed with GLM-5.3-Flash (via OpenCode Go, frames mode @ 12 frames)

This video is an animated, map-based history explainer about the **Louisiana Purchase**, using cute chibi-style cartoon characters of historical figures (Thomas Jefferson, James Monroe, and Napoleon Bonaparte) over stylized maps.

## Frame-by-Frame Summary

**00:04 — Introduction / Scene Setting**
The video opens on a map of Europe with France highlighted in blue. Two American envoys in 18th-century dress (representing Jefferson's diplomats) stand in France facing a scowling cartoon Napoleon in his bicorne hat and military uniform. A projection screen between them shows a map of North America, with a red dot marking a location. Subtitle: *"and offer to buy"* — establishing the premise: the Americans are in Paris to negotiate a purchase.

**00:12 — Rewind to the Beginning**
The scene shifts to the North Atlantic, with the eastern United States highlighted in blue. The two American characters sail eastward in a small red sailing ship, with arrows pointing back toward the U.S. coast, indicating a journey across the ocean. Subtitle: *"from the beginning"* — signaling a flashback to explain how the situation arose.

**00:21 — Colonial Context**
A wide Atlantic view shows British possessions in North America (Canada) in red, the United States in teal, and France in blue in Europe. The two Americans are now standing in Britain. Subtitle: *"a huge territory"* — setting up discussion of colonial landholdings.

**00:29 — The Caribbean Empires**
The map now labels the powers: **Britain** (red, Canada), **USA** (teal), **Spain** (orange, Florida/Mexico/Central America), with the Louisiana Territory in blue on the left edge. The Caribbean islands are shown colored by colonial power. Subtitle: *"richest colony"* — introducing France's most valuable holding.

**00:37 — Saint-Domingue's Wealth**
A close-up of the island of Hispaniola (Haiti/Saint-Domingue) overlaid with icons of sugar cubes, coffee beans, and sugarcane. Subtitle: *"to hundreds of thousands"* — referencing the plantation economy and enslaved population.

**00:46 — French Saint-Domingue**
The island is shown colored blue and labeled **"France"** — this is the colony of Saint-Domingue (Haiti). Subtitle: *"France basically"* — emphasizing how central this colony was to French interests.

**00:54 — The Haitian Revolution**
Live-action background footage of a tropical beach with mountains, overlaid with cartoon French soldiers in blue uniforms and shako hats carrying muskets, some fading/blurring as if falling. Subtitle: *"were successful"* — depicting the Haitian Revolution, where the enslaved rebellion defeated French forces.

**01:03 — Napoleon and Louisiana**
A map of North America with **Britain** (red), **Louisiana Territory** (blue), **USA** (teal), and **Spain** (orange). A realistic portrait of Napoleon appears on the left. Subtitle: *"suddenly made"* — connecting the loss of Haiti to Napoleon's changing calculus about Louisiana.

**01:11 — New Orleans' Importance**
A close-up on the Mississippi River glowing as it flows through the Louisiana Territory down to the port city of **New Orleans**, marked with a small city icon and trade goods (crates, a barrel) at its mouth. Subtitle: *"through New Orleans"* — showing the river's vital role in American trade.

**01:19 — Diplomatic Pressure**
The U.S. East Coast map with a character representing Thomas Jefferson (white curly hair, black coat) standing in American territory, facing another figure off to the right. Subtitle: *"With war against Britain"* — explaining the geopolitical pressure driving the deal.

**01:28 — The Deal**
Back in Europe: the two American envoys face a now-smiling Napoleon, who gestures toward a blue-highlighted shape (the Louisiana Territory) on a display board. A silver briefcase sits between them, symbolizing the payment. Subtitle: *"for fifteen"* — referencing the $15 million price.

**01:36 — The Result**
Final wide map of North America: the United States is now shown in white, expanded to include the entire Louisiana Territory, nearly doubling its size, with Britain (red) to the north and Spain (orange) to the south and west. Subtitle: *"almost doubling the size"* — concluding with the outcome of the purchase.

## Overall Narrative

The video explains how the Haitian Revolution destroyed France's most profitable colony and, combined with looming war with Britain and American dependence on the Mississippi River/New Orleans trade, led Napoleon to sell the entire Louisiana Territory to the United States for $15 million in 1803 — nearly doubling the nation's size.
